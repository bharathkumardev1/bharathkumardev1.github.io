Ask a 10-K a question

This searches real SEC filings with the retrieval pipeline from finance-rag-eval. It reads the company and period out of your question, narrows to those filings, ranks their pages with BM25, and sends questions about a balance sheet, income statement or cash flow figure to those statement pages first. It all runs in your browser.

  • (a question the pipeline gets wrong)

Pick an example or type a question. The first search fetches 111 KB of index data, then about 170 KB of compressed page text for each filing it opens.

    The demo holds the 84 filings that FinanceBench questions cite, out of the benchmark's 360. Checked against the Python pipeline on every benchmark question it can search (146 of 150, 438 question and setting pairs): same top 10 pages in the same order, same scores to the last bit. The two switches turn off the last two steps in the table below, so you can see what each one does to a single question.

    What the pipeline is worth, measured

    FinanceBench has 150 analyst questions over 360 filings (53,399 pages), and each question is labeled with the page that answers it. That makes retrieval measurable without grading generated answers: did the right page make the top 10? I split the questions once into 50 for development and 100 held out. Everything I tuned, I tuned on the 50; the table is the 100.

    Filings share most of their vocabulary, so BM25 over everything finds a page from the right filing only 42% of the time. The filter reads the company, year and quarter out of the question. It recognized the company in 97 of the 100 held-out questions, narrowed 360 filings to 2.5 on average, and took Hit@10 from 0.14 to 0.43 (+0.29, 95% interval +0.20 to +0.38).

    Routing is worth as much again, and it's a small piece of domain knowledge. The page detector marks 96 of the 168 labeled answer pages as a balance sheet, income statement or cash flow statement: tables of numbers that share almost no words with "what is the FY2019 fixed asset turnover ratio". Questions that name a statement, or a line item or ratio that lives on one, get those pages first. 62 of the 100 held-out questions trigger it, and Hit@10 went from 0.43 to 0.71 (+0.28, 95% interval +0.19 to +0.37).

    Leaving the company's name out of the query is the step I'm least sure of. Without routing it hurts (0.43 to 0.33), because the name is one of the few words a statement page shares with the question. With routing it adds +0.04, 95% interval −0.02 to +0.10, which is within noise.

    The embedding model didn't earn its cost. Fused with BM25, MiniLM scored 0.73 against BM25's 0.75 on the held-out questions (−0.02, 95% interval −0.08 to +0.03) and takes 15 ms a query instead of 1.7. On the 50 development questions it looked better (0.78 against 0.66), which is why it's the configuration I committed to before testing; a gap that size on 50 questions turned out to be noise. Without the filter MiniLM does beat BM25 (0.24 against 0.14), because it finds the right filing more often (61% against 42%). The filter makes that redundant.

    Where it still misses

    Each held-out question whose answer page didn't make the top 10 is sorted into the first stage of the pipeline that lost it. The rules run on every evaluation, so the counts change when the code does. The biggest group, narrative pages that share almost no words with the question, is where a cross-encoder reranker would go next.

    Who to call after discharge

    At Cigna I judged the models by how many high-need patients a fixed outreach capacity of 1,000 a month would identify, alongside AUC. readmission-capacity is the same problem on public data: 101,766 diabetic inpatient encounters from 130 US hospitals (UCI, 1999 to 2008), split by patient, with death and hospice discharges removed. On 19,745 held-out encounters, a LightGBM call list the size of 10% of discharges reaches 24.9% of the patients who come back within 30 days, against 20.5% when ranking by prior inpatient visits and 15.8% with a LACE score.

    Score a patient

    The trees run in your browser and give the same scores as LightGBM, which the repo tests on 2,000 held-out rows. Inputs not on the form are held at the held-out set's median or most common value. The list has a blind spot: 41% of the readmissions were patients with no inpatient, emergency or outpatient visit in the year before, and the 10% list reaches only 12% of them.

    Other projects

    Agentic Research Assistant

    Answers questions about a set of research papers with a citation on every claim. A retriever, a summarizer and an evaluator run in a LangGraph loop over one FAISS index until the evaluator is satisfied or a hard budget of model calls runs out. It's a tested FastAPI service with auth, per-caller rate limits, timeouts and Prometheus metrics; the whole graph runs offline against a stub model in CI. What it doesn't have yet is a published answer-quality evaluation, which needs an API budget I haven't spent.
